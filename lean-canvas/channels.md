# Channels

Path to customers.

## Summary
The short answer that goes into the [canvas](lean-canvas.md).

- **Now: direct, founder-led sales** to early adopters (pharma HTE groups, agrochemical majors). Run paid pilots on their own reactions.
- **Papers and public benchmarks**: prove the "most accurate" claim in the open; the founder's research reputation brings inbound interest.
- **Network**: Microsoft Research, University of Amsterdam, investors' portfolio companies.
- **Later: partnerships** with CROs (e.g., WuXi, Enamine) and HTE hardware makers (e.g., Unchained Labs, Mettler-Toledo), plus a free web demo for chemists.

## Research

All channels are hypotheses; test them with the first pilots.

| Channel | Stage | Why it fits | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder-led sales | Now | Few, large customers; trust matters; the founder can talk chemistry and ML | Doesn't scale; long pharma sales cycles |
| Paid pilots on customer reactions | Now | Proves value on their data; the result is measurable (first-try success rate) | Data-sharing and IP terms slow the start |
| Papers and benchmarks | Now | Credibility with scientists; inbound leads; recruiting | Competitors learn from what we publish |
| Conferences (ACS, SLAS, Gordon conferences) | Now | Where HTE and process chemists meet | Slow and costly per lead |
| Network (Microsoft, University of Amsterdam, investors) | Now | Warm introductions | Limited reach |
| CRO partnerships | Later | One deal reaches many molecules; CROs publish volumes and success rates | CROs want proof first |
| HTE hardware partnerships | Later | Our model plans the experiments their robots run | They may build their own software |
| Free web demo | Later | Chemists try it themselves; generates leads and data | Support cost; gives away value |

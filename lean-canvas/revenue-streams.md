# Revenue Streams

How the company makes money.

## Summary
The short answer that goes into the [canvas](lean-canvas.md).

1. **Paid pilots** on customers' own reactions (price to be tested in customer calls).
2. **Enterprise license** after a successful pilot (annual; Schrödinger is the template).
3. **Outcome-based pricing**: pay per validated route that works in the lab.
4. **Later, upside**: partnerships with upfront fee, milestones and royalties.

### Business-model options
| Model | Typical size | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seat or self-serve SaaS | $1k–75k per year | Recurring, scalable | Small contracts; pharma dislikes seat licensing ([Bessemer 2026](https://staging.bvp.com/atlas/roadmap-reinventing-life-sciences-with-ai)) [S] |
| Enterprise platform license | Up to ~$4M per year per large customer (Schrödinger) | Predictable, high retention | Needs a proven product and long sales cycles |
| Usage or credits | Thousands to tens of thousands per year [U] | Easy to start; matches compute cost | Unpredictable; risk of commoditization |
| Paid pilots, projects, FTE services | Pilot size: no public data; $50–500k is a guess to test [U] | Fast cash; builds trust and data | Limited by people and lab capacity |
| Per deliverable (compound, route) | $20–244 per compound (Enamine) | Price tied to the outcome | Low price per unit; needs capacity |
| Partnership (upfront + milestones + royalties) | Upfront $12–150M; headline $0.26–5.2B, mostly contingent on milestones | Largest sums; validates the technology | Needs pipeline-grade results; pharma keeps value in milestones (McKinsey 2026) [S] |
| Own pipeline | Very costly (Recursion: −$645M per year) | Largest upside | Highest risk |

### Takeaways
- Hybrid models (software + services + partnership upside) do best commercially ([Excelra 2026](https://excelra.com/publications/state-of-ai-ml-in-drug-discovery-2026/)) [S].
- Seat-based SaaS for retrosynthesis is small; most commercial prices aren't even public.
- Typical path: paid pilots → enterprise license (Schrödinger is the template).
- "Works on the first try" fits outcome-based pricing, e.g., per validated route, as Enamine prices per compound with stated success rates.
- Partnerships are upside, not the base: most of the value is in milestones that are years away.
- Gaps: pilot sizes, time to close, and most commercial prices are not public; ask buyers in customer calls.
